What each check does

Content, context and user experience are the three things the case asks us to test. We don't trust any one method alone, because each catches a different kind of error.

CheckTestsHowCatches
CompletenessContentAlign sentences between original and translation, then compare counts and lengths.A missing disclosure or a skipped reply.
Back-translationContentTranslate the English back to the source language and compare embeddings with the original.Meaning that drifted. It is weak on tone and small number changes, so it never runs alone.
Key banking wordsContentGlossary lookup both ways, such as CELI ↔ TFSA and 按揭 ↔ mortgage.Wrong product names. It also flags important keywords for human review.
Numbers and entitiesContentExtract amounts, rates, dates and phone numbers from both sides and compare the sets.A changed figure, which is the riskiest error.
Speaker attributionContextCompare speaker labels and turn order line by line.A customer's words shown as the bot's, or turns out of order.
Untranslated leftoversExperienceLanguage detection on every sentence, plus a script check for Chinese.Half-translated lines and English fallback.
Names and departmentsContentA protected list of people, product and department names.A department name turned into a meaningless phrase.
LLM judgeContext, ExperienceFixed rubric, structured output, calibrated against native reviewers.Wrong tone or formality, lost context across turns, a summary that misstates the chat.

Scoring and routing

Score = 0.5 × Content + 0.3 × Context + 0.2 × Experience
ParityL = average Score in language Laverage Score in English

Weights are a starting proposal. Parity of 1.0 means the language performs as well as English.

  • Pass. All checks are clean, so the conversation is logged to the dashboard.
  • Suspicious. Low similarity, a flagged keyword or an unsure judge sends it to a human, with the reason shown.
  • Fail. A number, entity or required sentence is wrong. A human always reviews these, whatever the score.
