How PolyBot checks every language

A daily job samples conversations in French, Cantonese and Mandarin, runs seven automatic checks on each one against its English version, and sends only the suspicious ones to a human.

What we test

Content

Facts match the English. Numbers, banking terms and meaning are the same, and nothing is dropped or added.

Context

The conversation stays intact: who said what, in what order, and what it was about.

User experience

It reads like a native speaker wrote it. The reply is in the right language and tone, with no stray English, and names appear the way a client expects.

The flow in four steps

Every conversation ends as a pass or as a case for a human reviewer.

Reviewer verdicts go back to step 2, where they tune the thresholds and calibrate the judge. Over time fewer cases need a human.

The seven checks in step 2

We don't trust any one method alone, because each catches a different kind of error.

Completeness

Content

No sentence is dropped or merged away.

How Align sentences between original and translation, then compare counts and lengths.

Catches A missing disclosure or a skipped reply.

Back-translation

Content

Translate back, measure semantic similarity, record the metric.

How Translate the English back to the source language and compare embeddings with the original.

Catches Meaning that drifted. It is weak on tone and small number changes, so it never runs alone.

Key banking words

Content

Banking terms map to the approved equivalent.

How Glossary lookup both ways, such as CELI ↔ TFSA and 按揭 ↔ mortgage.

Catches Wrong product names. It also flags important keywords for human review.

Numbers and entities

Content

Every amount, rate, date and phone number matches exactly.

How Extract them from both sides with patterns and compare the sets.

Catches A changed figure, which is the riskiest error.

Speaker attribution

Context

Each line belongs to the same speaker in both versions.

How Compare speaker labels and turn order line by line.

Catches A customer's words shown as the bot's, or turns out of order.

Untranslated leftovers

Experience

No source-language text is left in the English, and no English in the reply.

How Language detection on every sentence, plus a script check for Chinese.

Catches Half-translated lines and English fallback.

Names and departments

Content

Proper nouns keep their original form or map to the official name. A literal translation gets flagged.

How A protected list of people, product and department names.

Catches A department name turned into a meaningless phrase.

Plus an LLM judge

LLM judge

Context, Experience

It reads the original directly and answers narrow questions, quoting the words it relied on.

How Fixed rubric, structured output, calibrated against native reviewers.

Catches Wrong tone or formality, lost context across turns, a summary that misstates the chat.

A French conversation, checked

Each run works on three outputs: the original transcript, its English translation, and an English summary. This example is made up.

Customer, FrenchEnglish translation

J'ai perdu ma carte de crédit.

I lost my credit card.

Bot, FrenchEnglish translation

Je bloque votre carte tout de suite. Votre nouvelle carte arrivera dans 5 jours ouvrables.

I'm blocking your card right away. Your new card will arrive in 7 business days.

English summary

Customer reported a lost credit card. The bot blocked it and said a replacement arrives in 7 business days.

Scoring and routing

Score = 0.5 × Content + 0.3 × Context + 0.2 × Experience
ParityL = average Score in language Laverage Score in English

Weights are a starting proposal. Parity of 1.0 means the language performs as well as English.

Pass

All checks are clean, so the conversation is logged to the dashboard.

Suspicious

Low similarity, a flagged keyword or an unsure judge sends it to a human, with the reason shown.

Fail

A number, entity or required sentence is wrong. A human always reviews these, whatever the overall score.
