How PolyBot checks every language

A daily job samples conversations in French, Cantonese and Mandarin, runs seven automatic checks on each one against its English version, and sends only the suspicious ones to a human.

What we test

Three things have to hold in every language

Content

The facts survive

Numbers, banking terms and meaning are the same as in English. Nothing is dropped or added.

Context

The thread holds

Who said what, in what order, and what the conversation was about all stay intact.

User experience

It reads as native

Right language, right tone, no stray English, and names handled the way a client expects.

Flow and architecture

Four stages, with one feedback loop

Conversations enter on the left and leave on the right as either a pass or a case for a human reviewer.

01 · COLLECT

Gather conversations

  • Daily sampleA scheduled job grabs a set % of conversation pairs (original + English) from each language.
  • Scenario runsPolyBot generates test scenarios and a simulated customer chats with the bot, to cover paths real traffic has not hit.
IN: transcripts · English test cases · language packs
02 · VERIFY

Run the checks

Seven rule-based methods compare the original with its English translation. An LLM judge then reads what rules cannot: tone, context and the summary.

IN: banking glossary · English reference answers
03 · SCORE

Turn checks into a metric

Each check returns pass or flag plus a score. They roll up into one score per conversation and one parity number per language against English.

OUT: score per conversation · parity per language
04 · ROUTE

Pass, or send to a human

Clean conversations are logged to the dashboard. Anything suspicious goes to a native-speaking reviewer with the reason highlighted.

OUT: dashboard · review queue · release gate
Feedback loop. Reviewer verdicts flow back to stage 02 to tune thresholds and calibrate the judge, so fewer cases need a human over time.
Inside stage 02 · the translation layer

Seven checks, plus a judge for what rules miss

No single method is trusted alone. Each one catches a different kind of error.

01Content

Completeness

No sentence is dropped or merged away.

How
Align sentences between original and translation, then compare counts and lengths.
Catches
A missing disclosure or a skipped reply.
02Content

Back-translation

translate back→semantic similarity→metric
How
Translate the English back to the source language and compare embeddings with the original.
Catches
Meaning that drifted. Weak on tone and small number changes, so it never runs alone.
03Content

Key banking words

Banking terms map to the approved equivalent.

How
Glossary lookup both ways, such as CELI ↔ TFSA and 按揭 ↔ mortgage.
Catches
Wrong product names. Important keywords are flagged for human review.
04Content

Numbers and entities

Every amount, rate, date and phone number matches exactly.

How
Extract them from both sides with patterns and compare the sets.
Catches
The highest-risk error: a changed figure.
05Context

Speaker attribution

Each line belongs to the same speaker in both versions.

How
Compare speaker labels and turn order line by line.
Catches
A customer's words shown as the bot's, or turns out of order.
06Experience

Untranslated leftovers

No source-language text is left in the English, and no English in the reply.

How
Language detection on every sentence, plus a script check for Chinese.
Catches
Half-translated lines and English fallback.
07Content

Names and departments

Proper nouns are kept or mapped to their official name, never translated literally.

How
A protected list of people, product and department names.
Catches
A department name turned into a meaningless phrase.
+ JUDGEContext · Experience

LLM judge

Reads the original directly and answers narrow questions with quoted evidence.

How
Fixed rubric, structured output, calibrated against native reviewers.
Catches
Wrong tone or formality, lost context across turns, a summary that misstates the chat.

Stages 03 and 04

How a conversation is scored and routed

Score

Score = 0.5 × Content + 0.3 × Context + 0.2 × Experience
ParityL = average Score in language Laverage Score in English

Weights are a starting proposal. Parity of 1.0 means the language performs as well as English.

Route

  • PassAll checks clean. Logged to the dashboard.
  • SuspiciousLow similarity, a flagged keyword or an unsure judge. Goes to a human with the reason shown.
  • FailA number, entity or required sentence is wrong. Always reviewed, whatever the overall score.
Still to decide

Three open questions for the team

  • Client data. The daily job reads real conversations, so it must run inside RBC's environment with personal details masked.
  • Sample size. What % of conversations per language is enough to trust the daily number?
  • Thresholds. The similarity cut-off for a suspicious case should be set from a native-labelled sample, not guessed.
