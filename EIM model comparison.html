EIM chatbot: GPT-5 vs Haiku 5.5 vs Sonnet 5.5

Pros and cons when the goal is escalate rather than hallucinate · Nov 19, 2026 · Hitesh Malhotra · Based on one run per model, 43 golden questions, reseeded local KB (script 07 output)
The standard used here. A good escalation happens when the retrieved sources do not support an answer. A wrong confident answer is worse than an unnecessary escalation, so missed escalations (answering when it should have escalated) and unsupported claims count most against a model. Fewer escalations is not a pro. Neither has been measured yet: nobody has labelled which questions are answerable from the sources.

Bottom line (provisional)

No model can be called safest yet. The data only shows how often each escalates, not whether it was right. On behavior alone, Sonnet and GPT-5 lean cautious (21 and 19 escalations) and Haiku is the one to verify: it answered Q2 and Q22 where both others escalated, and those answers could be good coverage or hallucinations. Haiku is also the most transparent when it does escalate (it states what the sources do not cover). Haiku also has a latency problem to rule in or out.

Decision rule to agree with the manager: a model passes only if (1) it has no more missed escalations than GPT-5, (2) it has zero fact contradictions, (3) its unsupported-claim rate is no higher than GPT-5's. Pick between passing models on false escalations, latency and cost.

Per model

GPT-5 (comparator, being deprecated)

Escalates 19 of 43
Pros
  • Cautious: it escalates on 19 questions and gives no partial guess.
  • Steady latency (p90 9.7 s, max 12.1 s).
  • No citation errors; the SME preferred its earlier responses.
Cons
  • Escalation message is canned, so the user learns nothing about what the sources cover.
  • Escalations doubled versus its own baseline; part may be noise or the KB, not caution.
  • Whether its answers are all supported by the sources has not been checked.
  • Being deprecated, so it is only the yardstick.

Haiku 5.5

Escalates 17 of 43
Pros
  • Most useful escalations: says what the sources do cover and what is not covered, then sets the escalate flag (14 of 17).
  • Content equals GPT-5's when both answer (cosine difference about 0).
  • No citation errors.
  • Probably the cheapest (not measured).
Cons
  • Answered Q2 and Q22 where GPT-5 and Sonnet escalated: possible missed escalations until checked against the sources.
  • Partial answers on escalation may break the system prompt's escalation rule, and could reach users as if they were confirmed.
  • Latency tail: p90 40.4 s, max 72.2 s.
To verify
  • Q2, Q22: do the sources support its answers?
  • Does every hedged answer carry the escalate flag?
  • Does the 40–72 s tail repeat?

Sonnet 5.5

Escalates 21 of 43
Pros
  • Most conservative by count: the least likely to give an unsupported confident answer (to be confirmed).
  • Content equals GPT-5's when both answer (cosine difference about 0).
  • Steady latency (p90 11.2 s, max 13.8 s); no citation errors.
Cons
  • Escalated Q3 and Q15, which GPT-5 and Haiku answered: false escalations if the sources support an answer.
  • Canned message only; the user gets no partial help.
  • Likely the most expensive (not measured).
To verify
  • Q3, Q15: are they answerable from the sources?

What would settle it (about 14 questions, an hour)

  1. Model disagreements: Q2, Q3, Q11, Q15, Q22, Q42.
  2. Shared new escalations the baseline answered: Q4, Q18, Q23, Q28, Q30, Q31, Q34, Q43. These are only wrong if the sources support an answer; missing documents could make them correct escalations.
  3. For each, read the retrieved sources (or the golden answer) and label it Answerable, Partial or Not answerable. Then mark each model's behavior: justified escalation, false escalation, missed escalation, or supported answer.
  4. For every answer given, check each stated fact against the sources (unsupported claims, contradictions).
  5. Repeat each model at least twice (temperature is not set) so one-question differences are not mistaken for real ones.

Script 08 builds the scoring workbook (`answerable`, critical facts, hits, contradictions); its summary sheet counts false and missed escalations per model.

Caveats

  • One run per model and 43 questions: differences of 1–3 questions can be noise.
  • The reseeded KB is not the baseline KB (GPT-5 escalations went from 9 to 19). Some baseline-cited documents may be absent from the new KB.
  • Cosine to the reference cannot detect a wrong fact, and the reference is mostly GPT-5's own earlier output.
  • Cost, hallucination rate and prompt-rule compliance are not measured yet.
